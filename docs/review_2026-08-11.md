+0.0219, identical to structure-reuse alone). The only thing you give back is crossover's solo flow
improvement. That is a real trade, and it is why all three are installed rather than just one.

### `[CROSSOVER]` — we never crossed hands over; every human map does

| | crossover share | maps with none |
|---|---|---|
| human (150 maps) | median **0.183** | **0 of 150** |
| us, today | **0.0000** | **149 of 149** |
| `[CROSSOVER]` | 0.112 | 0 of 149 |

Our postprocess was shoving every "wrong side" note back to its own half, so red never played the
right side and blue never played the left. Humans do it on ~18 % of notes as a deliberate device.
Turning that down: **flow improves 0.37 → 0.23**, and two reachability measures land on the human
value (`reach_p90` 3.16 → **3.61**, human 3.61; `hard_rate` 0.049 → **0.062**, human 0.062) — we were
reaching *less* than a human, and now we reach like one. Note counts are unchanged; only which side
a note sits on. ⚠️It is still short of the human rate (0.112 vs 0.183) and errs conservative on
purpose: **no map exceeds the human p90.**
⚠️**Honesty about the pedigree**: this lever was tested and ticked as a win back on 2026-07-27
(`idiom 1.84 → 0.30`) and then never made it into the defaults. I did not invent it; I found it by
chasing a blind spot and re-validated it at n=149.

**reuses the bars it already generated there** instead of deciding that passage again from scratch.

## ⚠️ READ THIS FIRST — what to distrust

1. **Two of the four songs barely changed.** Only where the music has clear, sustained repeats does
   the lever do anything. **Hunger and Fallen Kingdom are ~14 % copied** (93–97 % of note times are
   unchanged from BEFORE) — if they sound the same, that is correct, not a bug.
   ★**Digital Life Hacker and アリスブルー are the real test: ~71 % of bars copied**, only ~65 % of
   note times shared. **If you only have time for two, play those.**
2. **The intended change is "more repetitive."** On a musical repeat the map is now often *literally
   the same bar* — which is what you do, and what we never did. **If those songs feel monotonous,
   that is this lever overdosing, not a mystery.** The dose is one number and easy to turn down.
3. 🔴**Two playability axes say this costs something**: `flow` 0.37 → 0.70 and `idiom` 0.40 → 1.75,
   both past their bars. I am **not** treating that as proof it plays worse, for two reasons measured
   last night: those axes **cannot score a single map at all** (they compare cohort *distributions*;
   on one map every axis returns `nan`), and across the suite they agree with your one recorded
   verdict at **13/26 — a coin flip**. So they flag a risk, they do not settle it. **You do.**
4. **Playfeel and rhythm were damaged by an earlier version of this and are now fully recovered**, so
   if something feels wrong, `flow`/`idiom` is where I would expect it: note-to-note motion and cut
   direction conventions at the **seams** where a copied passage begins and ends.

## The question I actually need answered

Not "is it better" — **"does the repetition read as *intentional* or as *lazy*?"** A mapper copies a
chorus and then varies it; we currently copy it and do not vary it. If it reads as lazy, the fix is
variation-on-repeat, which is a different and easier problem than the one we just solved.

And separately, still open from before: **is Fallen Kingdom "empty" compared to what our model used
to do, or compared to what the song wants?** Four instruments have failed to explain that word and
I would rather have your answer than build a fifth.

## 🔴 LATE ADDITION — look at `structure/` before you trust anything above

I rendered the structure panels (`structure/*.png`) and they changed my confidence.

- **Digital Life Hacker AFTER**: our panel gains large blocks that line up with the music's. This is
  the intended effect, and it is clearly visible.
- 🔴**アリスブルー AFTER**: our panel is a **rigid periodic checkerboard** — a regular grid of
  identical squares. The music's panel is irregular; the **human's** is irregular and rich. Ours is
  repeating *mechanically*, not following the song.

**So on the highest-dose songs this may be overdosed into a degenerate**, and the masterpiece axes
would not have told me — musical repeats are often periodic themselves, so a map that repeats at a
fixed lag scores well on "does the map repeat where the music repeats" without following the music.

⇒**This is precisely why I want your ear rather than my numbers.** If アリスブルー feels mechanical,
that is the checkerboard and the fix is simply less of it (the dose is one number). If it feels good
anyway, that is much more interesting and I would want to know why.
