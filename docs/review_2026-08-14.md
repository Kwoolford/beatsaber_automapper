## What the lever does

Our beat grid is anchored at **t = 0** — the first audio sample — rather than at the song's actual
first beat. On a song whose downbeat does not land at t=0, every note is offset by up to half a slot
for the whole song. The lever searches for the time shift that best lands our notes on the music's
onsets, and **refuses to move the map at all** unless it finds a clear gain.

## What to distrust

1. ★**The gain is partly circular and the suite cannot fix that.** The lever optimises our notes
   against the same onset detections the alignment score is measured with. Some of the improvement is
   therefore guaranteed by construction. What is *not* circular: no song got worse, the timing scatter
   tightened, and we **approach the human's level without exceeding it** — which is what you would
   expect from a real fix and not from fitting noise. **Your ear is the only thing that settles it.**
2. **I checked whether we are just tuning our own onset detector** — if we were, the human maps would
   want the same corrections. Against a permutation null they mostly do not (agreement 40.5 % vs
   37.8 % expected by chance, p = 0.32). My first version of this check said 59.5 % and looked
   alarming; its threshold sat exactly on its own noise floor, so it was measuring nothing.
3. **These are songs neither of us has heard the model attempt before**, so you have no baseline for
   "is this song's map good" the way you do for Hunger. **The question is narrower**: does `[PHASE]`
   feel more locked to the beat than `[BEFORE]`?

## The question

**Does `[PHASE]` sit on the beat better than `[BEFORE]`?** Not "is it a good map" — these are corpus
songs and the map underneath is our ordinary output. Only: **does it feel locked to the music, or
does it feel shifted off it?**

If the answer is yes, the default flips on. If you cannot tell, say so — that is also an answer, and
it means a measured 0.62 → 0.35 axis improvement is inaudible, which is worth knowing about the axis.
